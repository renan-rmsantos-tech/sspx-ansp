# ADR-004: Confirmed residence fields and historical CEP enrichment

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-13

## Context

`donor_pledges` stores free-text `endereco` and `cep` but no separate residence city or state. The PRD requires confirmed residence for new registrations and conservative classification of historical records.

## Decision

Add nullable `residence_city` and `residence_state` columns. The public form pre-fills them from ViaCEP when available but requires the registrant to confirm or correct them before submission. Run a bounded, resumable historical enrichment using existing CEP values and ViaCEP. Write only valid city and UF pairs returned for exact eight-digit CEPs; keep failed, incomplete, or ambiguous records null and report them as “Local não identificado.” Never infer residence from `priorado_capela`.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Parse the free-text address

- **Description**: Extract city and UF from `endereco` with text patterns.
- **Pros**: No external lookup.
- **Cons**: Addresses vary and may contain misleading abbreviations.
- **Why rejected**: Reliability is insufficient for the report.

### Alternative 2: Leave all historical residences unknown

- **Description**: Classify only new registrations.
- **Pros**: Simplest and safest migration.
- **Cons**: Discards reliable CEP-based location information.
- **Why rejected**: The operator chose CEP enrichment.

## Consequences

### Positive

- New residence values are explicit and historical records with valid CEPs can contribute to reports.

### Negative

- Historical enrichment depends on ViaCEP availability and rate limits.

### Risks

- A CEP lookup may not reflect a person's actual residence at registration time. Keep the derived values distinguishable from user-confirmed values and do not overwrite confirmed data.

## Implementation Notes

Add a source marker or equivalent provenance for `confirmed` versus `cep_lookup`. Keep the enrichment out of the startup migration and bootstrap path.
