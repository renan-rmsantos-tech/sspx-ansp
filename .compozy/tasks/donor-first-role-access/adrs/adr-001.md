# ADR-001: Adopt a donor-first experience with a safe route transition

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-31

## Context

The public experience currently gives scholarship applications greater prominence than donor enrollment, while the requested product direction is to make becoming a benefactor the primary public action. The same change introduces clearer public URLs and several related landing-page corrections. Existing bookmarks and previously shared links may still point to the old addresses.

## Decision

Adopt an integrated donor-first experience across the public navigation, hero, content order, and quick-access links. Keep scholarship applications visible as the secondary public action. Introduce the new public addresses `/seja-um-benfeitor` and `/solicitar-bolsa` while preserving a safe transition for visitors who use `/benfeitor` or `/form`.

The same release will align the approved logo across user-facing pages, activate scholarship document uploads, update the institutional email to `contato@arcaprovidencia.com.br`, remove the public administrative shortcut, and ensure the FSSPX name is never visually clipped in the footer.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Minimal public adjustment

- **Description**: Change only the navigation order, primary button text, and public URLs while retaining the current section hierarchy.
- **Pros**: Smaller visible change and lower content-review effort.
- **Cons**: Leaves conflicting signals about which action is primary and may not satisfy the requested donor emphasis.
- **Why rejected**: The user selected a coherent donor-first hierarchy across the complete landing experience.

### Alternative 2: Expanded operational portal

- **Description**: Combine the donor-first work with new queues, filters, and workflow tools for the Secretariat.
- **Pros**: Could support a broader future operational workflow.
- **Cons**: Adds capabilities not requested and increases validation and training needs.
- **Why rejected**: The extra workflow scope does not contribute directly to the requested release.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Public calls to action communicate one clear priority.
- Scholarship applicants still have an obvious path without competing for primary emphasis.
- Previously distributed public links remain useful during the route transition.
- Related identity, contact, upload, and footer defects are resolved as one coherent public-experience update.

### Negative

- More landing-page surfaces must be reviewed together for consistent hierarchy and copy.
- The release must account for both old and new public addresses.

### Risks

- Visitors may interpret donor emphasis as removal of scholarship support; retain a visible secondary scholarship action.
- Existing external references may use old addresses; keep continuity and verify all owned links.
- Visual changes may diverge across pages; use the approved Open Design logo source and validate each user-facing surface.

## Implementation Notes

Implementation design belongs in the TechSpec. Product acceptance must cover desktop and mobile public navigation, complete CTA hierarchy, old-link continuity, and the footer identity block.
