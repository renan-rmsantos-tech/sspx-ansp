# ADR-002: Separate Administrator and Secretariat capabilities by job responsibility

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-08-31

## Context

The current authenticated panel represents a single administrative persona with access to every application detail and every decision or configuration action. The organization needs a Secretariat profile that can perform follow-up work without approving applications or accessing unnecessary financial declarations, submitted evidence, decision rationale, or awarded percentages.

## Decision

Introduce two staff profiles with explicit product capabilities:

- **Administrator** has access to every existing and new administrative capability, including approval and rejection, configuration, final-document issuance, and Secretariat account administration.
- **Secretariat** has a restricted operational view containing applicant and student names, school, parent contact details, the final approved or rejected result, internal Secretariat notes, benefactor enrollments submitted through the public donor form, and final documents already issued by an Administrator.

Secretariat users cannot access declared income and expenses, evidence uploaded by applicants, decision rationale, awarded discount percentages, or approval and rejection controls. They cannot issue or reissue final documents. Secretariat notes form a dated internal history, are readable by Administrators, and never appear to families or in final documents.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Full application visibility without decision controls

- **Description**: Show Secretariat users the same application and document data as Administrators but hide approval and rejection actions.
- **Pros**: Minimal difference between staff experiences.
- **Cons**: Exposes sensitive information that the Secretariat does not need for its stated job.
- **Why rejected**: It conflicts with the agreed operational view and data-minimization goal.

### Alternative 2: Result-only access after a decision

- **Description**: Let Secretariat users see only decided applications, basic identity data, and the result.
- **Pros**: Strongest information restriction.
- **Cons**: Prevents the Secretariat from maintaining internal follow-up notes and preparing contact workflows consistently.
- **Why rejected**: The user requires notes, parent contacts, school context, benefactor data, and access to final documents.

### Alternative 3: Shared Secretariat account

- **Description**: Use one credential for all Secretariat staff.
- **Pros**: Simple initial account setup.
- **Cons**: Weak accountability and difficult access revocation when staff changes.
- **Why rejected**: The user selected Administrator-managed individual Secretariat accounts.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Staff see only the information and actions required for their responsibilities.
- Approval authority remains exclusively with Administrators.
- Individual Secretariat accounts improve accountability and access lifecycle management.
- Internal follow-up context is preserved without leaking into family-facing documents.

### Negative

- Every authenticated surface and action must have an explicit capability assignment.
- Administrators gain responsibility for managing Secretariat accounts.
- The product must clearly distinguish submitted evidence from final issued documents.

### Risks

- Hiding controls without enforcing the same restrictions everywhere would create false security; authorization acceptance must cover direct access as well as navigation.
- Ambiguous labels could expose the wrong document category; distinguish applicant uploads from final issued documents in staff language.
- Notes may contain unnecessary sensitive content; provide concise guidance that they are operational and internal.

## Implementation Notes

The TechSpec must define a complete capability matrix and verification coverage for every authenticated page, action, and document access path. Unspecified capabilities default to unavailable for the Secretariat.
