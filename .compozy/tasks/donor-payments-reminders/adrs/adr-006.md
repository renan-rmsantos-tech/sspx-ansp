# ADR-006: Use fixed public due-day choices and transactional instructions

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-26

## Context

The completed phase-1 form accepts any monthly day from 1 to 28 and shows Pix details during registration. The operator now requires five deliberate public choices, with day 30 supported, while administrators may use any day from 1 to 31. Monthly donors should receive payment instructions by email after registration whether or not they opt into scheduled reminders. The form and reminder phases must ship together before publication.

## Decision

Require a public monthly day choice of 1, 10, 15, 20, or 30, with no preselection. Validate this set on the server. Keep the legacy backfill capped at 28, but relax the database and administrator edit range to 1–31. Retain the chosen or edited day in the pledge; for each competence, calculate the actual due date as the lesser of that day and the last day of the month. Derive 10-day and 5-day reminder dates from the actual due date, never from the nominal stored day. The first due date is the first adjusted date on or after registration in São Paulo.

Show Pix instructions before date and required receipt only for one-time registration. For monthly registration, explain before submission that a separate transactional email will provide the Pix key, beneficiary, CNPJ, amount, chosen day, first due date, duration, month-end rule, required first-occurrence receipt link, and reminder policy. The monthly registration itself requests no receipt; each later monthly payment requires its own upload. Send the email to every newly registered monthly donor after the pledge transaction commits, independent of reminder opt-in and welcome email. Keep a database send-attempt history with a transactional pending row, short reservation, frozen payload, safe status, and explicit administrative resend generation. Do not send it automatically to historical monthly donors; allow manual send from the donor list. Registration succeeds despite an email failure.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Keep the free numeric public day

- **Pros**: Minimal form change.
- **Cons**: Does not offer the operator's five fixed choices and still prevents day 30.
- **Why rejected**: It fails the required public journey.

### Alternative 2: Normalize day 30 to 28 in storage

- **Pros**: Existing recurrence helper would need fewer changes.
- **Cons**: Donors would lose their chosen day in 30- and 31-day months, and reminders would use the wrong date.
- **Why rejected**: The adjustment belongs to each month's due date, not the pledge.

### Alternative 3: Treat instructions as an opted-in reminder

- **Pros**: Could reuse the daily reminder selection.
- **Cons**: Monthly donors who decline reminders would not receive the promised payment instructions.
- **Why rejected**: The instructions are transactional and independent of opt-in.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The public choice is clear while administrators retain flexibility to correct legacy and exceptional days.
- February and other short months have deterministic due and reminder dates without changing the stored day.
- New monthly donors have a visible, recoverable instructions-email state even when Resend fails.

### Negative

- The existing recurrence helper, admin edit, database check, public form, and related tests need a new pending task despite their completed baseline tasks.
- Explicit resends require a new attempt generation and clear status presentation.

### Risks

- A send can be accepted by Resend before its status is committed. An expired reservation after provider call becomes uncertain and needs administrator review, not automatic replay.
- A historical donor's first occurrence may already be past. Manual sends default to an eligible current/future receipt-pending occurrence, can target an overdue pending occurrence within ledger coverage with a renewed link, or show a clear no-link message when no eligible occurrence exists.
